VIDEO AND SOUND
Create one 16:9 animated clip for a preschool family role-play. Polished warm 3D family-animation style, soft pastel materials, warm light, expressive but restrained faces. A locked medium two-shot with both faces and task-relevant hands visible; use the scene-specific safe framing below when seats/props require it. No camera orbit, zoom, unrelated cutaways, montage, extra characters or extra voices. No music over speech, narrator, subtitles, captions, title cards or UI. A physical object label explicitly required by the scene is allowed. Clean natural English, exact speaker attribution, synchronized mouth movements; silent listener reacts without mouthing the other's line.

FIXED CAST (only these two)
Child — a four-year-old cartoon tiger boy, orange fur with black stripes, yellow T-shirt, short rounded toddler proportions; clear natural child voice.
Bear Peer — the same small brown bear in a teal T-shirt in every peer lesson; age four, child-sized proportions and a distinct natural child voice, never an adult.

EMOTIONAL PERFORMANCE
Calm, warm and regulated baseline. Both friends speak at ordinary play volume and keep relaxed seated postures.
Peer shows a small earnest look when requesting one final roll. Child considers and confirms the order, watches attentively and shows a small appreciative smile after receiving the ball; neither needs to look delighted about waiting.
No grabbing, sulking used to force agreement, pressured smiling, shouting, jumping frenzy, competitive cheering, frantic gestures or elastic faces. The ball moves slowly and stays on the mat during each roll.
No screaming, extreme excitement, rage, distorted facial or body shapes, frantic gestures or uncontrolled running.

SETTING
Two friends sit close together on a clear home play mat and share one ball.

SCENE AND PROP CONTINUITY
A clear home play mat with Bear Peer sitting screen left and Child sitting screen right, angled toward each other and close enough for a short hand-to-hand pass. Peer is the same small brown bear, age four, in his teal T-shirt; Child is the four-year-old tiger boy in his yellow T-shirt. Exactly one small red ball is present. Peer can roll it about twenty centimeters between his own hands in front of his knees; a separate clear short path between the friends will allow Child's later roll back. No truck, blocks, targets, extra balls or other props. Locked 16:9 medium two-shot keeps both faces, knees, hands and the full ball path visible.
Keep base character designs, garment identities, lighting, set and camera consistent across the three clips; allow the explicitly scripted dressing or undressing actions. The START FRAME below specifies this clip's current state; do not reset to the scene's initial arrangement. START FRAME
Peer steadies the one red ball under his right palm in front of his knees, with his left hand twenty centimeters away. Child sits close on the right with his hands resting on his own knees.

ACTION AND REACTIONS
Peer gently rolls the ball from his right palm into his waiting left hand during the opening turn, making his current play visible. The ball has stopped before Child requests the next turn. Peer holds it still as he asks for one additional roll; Child confirms the order and settles in to watch how Peer rolls it, leaving control with Peer. This opening roll precedes the agreement and is distinct from the single final roll in the next clip.

SPOKEN DIALOGUE (verbatim, in order):
Peer: "Watch me roll the red ball."
Child: "Can I have a turn after you?"
Peer: "Can I roll it once more first?"
Child: "You first, then me. I'll watch how you roll it."

END FRAME
The red ball is stopped beneath Peer's left palm in front of his knees. His right hand rests twenty centimeters away, and Child watches attentively with his hands on his own knees.
Leave a short natural settling beat, without adding speech. Do not repeat earlier clips' lines.
